# Investor Intel

Pre-pitch research that tells you whether an investor is worth your time, what they actually care about, and how to reach them in a way that gets a reply. Built from the reality of fundraising as a first-time founder — where most pitches fail not because the company is bad, but because the founder pitched the wrong person the wrong way.

Marina pitched 50+ investors before her first yes. This skill reflects what she learned about doing that research before the meeting, not after the rejection.

---

## WHAT TO PROVIDE

To run a full intel profile, provide one or more of:

- Investor name and fund
- Fund website or Crunchbase/LinkedIn URL
- Your company's one-liner (what you do, stage, sector)
- Any mutual connections or warm intros available
- The specific ask (cold email, meeting prep, fit assessment, target list)

---

## STEP 1: BUILD THE INVESTOR PROFILE

Research and summarize the following. If web search is available, fetch current data. If not, flag what needs manual verification.

### Portfolio and thesis
- **Fund stage focus:** pre-seed, seed, Series A+
- **Check size range** (typical first check)
- **Sector focus:** what do they actually back vs. what they say they back
- **Portfolio companies:** name 3–5 relevant ones, note what they have in common
- **Co-investors:** who do they typically syndicate with
- **Geographic bias:** US-only, global, specific markets

### The investor as a person
- **Recent public statements:** tweets, podcast appearances, blog posts from the last 6 months — what are they thinking about right now
- **Known thesis angles:** what problems do they believe are underserved
- **Red flags:** any known patterns — takes forever to decide, re-trades terms, goes dark after term sheet, not founder-friendly
- **Best path in:** direct cold, warm intro, event, portfolio founder referral

### Fit assessment
Rate fit on three dimensions (High / Medium / Low / Unknown):

| Dimension | Rating | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Stage fit | | Does your stage match their typical entry point? |
| Sector fit | | Is your space in their stated or revealed thesis? |
| Founder fit | | Do they back founders like you (background, geography, team size)? |

**If any dimension is Low, flag it explicitly before continuing.** A low-fit pitch wastes both sides' time.

## STEP 2: MEETING PREP (if preparing for a call)

Produce a one-page brief with:

**Opening hook** — one sentence connecting your company to something the investor has said publicly or a portfolio gap you fill. Specific beats flattering. "You backed Duolingo at seed — we're doing for [X] what Duolingo did for language" is stronger than "I'm a huge fan of your portfolio."

**Likely questions to prepare for:**
- Based on their known thesis, what will they push on hardest?
- What objections has this investor raised publicly about companies like yours?
- What's the bear case for your space that a smart investor in this sector would already know?

**What they probably want to see:**
- What metric or milestone would make this investor write a check? (Revenue? DAUs? Enterprise contracts? A specific co-investor?)
- What's their decision timeline — do they move fast or slow?

**What to ask them:**
- One question that demonstrates you've done the research: "I saw you posted about [X] last month — how does that shape how you think about [your space]?"
- One question about their process: "What does your path to conviction usually look like for a seed deal?"

---

## STEP 3: COLD OUTREACH DRAFT (if writing an email or DM)

Apply the founder-voice rules. Write like a founder, not like a pitch deck.

**Structure:**
1. **One specific hook** — reference something real: a portfolio company, a recent post, a mutual connection. Never generic flattery.
2. **One sentence on what you do** — specific, no jargon.
3. **One sentence on traction** — a real number. "We have 11 paying customers at $2K/month" beats "strong early traction."
4. **One clear ask** — a 20-minute call, not "would love to connect."
5. **Total length: under 100 words.** If it's longer, cut.

**What to avoid:**
- "I've long admired your portfolio" — delete
- "We're building the [X] for [Y]" as the opener — save analogies for the call
- Attaching a deck cold — offer to send it, don't attach
- CC'ing anyone — one name in the To field

**Cold email template:**
```
Subject: [Specific company they backed] + [your space]

[First name],

[One sentence hook — reference to their portfolio, thesis, or recent post.]

[What you do — one sentence, no jargon.]

[Traction — one specific number.]

Would a 20-minute call make sense? Happy to send the deck first if helpful.

[Your name]
```

---

## STEP 4: TARGET LIST (if building a list)

When asked to build a target investor list, output a table:

| Investor / Fund | Stage | Sector fit | Best path in | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank by: (1) stage fit, (2) sector fit, (3) warmth of path in.

Flag anyone on the list who has publicly passed on similar companies — they're low priority unless something has changed.

---

## RULES

- **Never pitch to a low-fit investor as a "warm-up."** Your reputation travels. A bad first impression with one partner at a fund affects the whole fund.
- **Research the person, not just the fund.** Partners at the same fund have different theses. Find the right partner, not just the right logo.
- **One ask per email.** Don't ask for a call and a deck review and feedback on your pitch. One thing.
- **If you don't have a warm path in, say so and build one.** A cold email to a shared portfolio founder asking for an intro converts better than any cold email to the investor directly.

---

## ONE-LINE SUMMARY

The best pitch meetings are won before you walk in the room — by knowing exactly who you're talking to, why they should care, and what would make them say yes.
